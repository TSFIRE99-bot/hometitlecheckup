What is a tangled title?

"Grandma's house is still in Grandpa's name."

When someone who owns a home dies, the home doesn't move to the family on its own. Someone has to file paperwork with the County or go through probate court. If nobody does, the family keeps living there, but legally the home still belongs to the person who died. That's called a tangled title (or heirs' property).

With every generation it gets harder to fix, because the ownership splits among more and more relatives:

The easy fix most people don't know about

Ohio has a Transfer on Death Designation Affidavit: a short form the owner signs in front of a notary and records with the County Recorder. When the owner dies, the home goes straight to the people named, with no probate. It has to be recorded while the owner is alive. Forms: {{ tod_link|md }}.

If the owner has already died, the family usually needs probate court. Small estates can use a shorter process. Check your home to see which steps fit your family.
