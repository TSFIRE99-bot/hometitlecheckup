# Sales kit (drafts for founder review)

Every message here is a draft. Nothing has been sent, no form has been submitted, and no call or meeting has been
made or booked. A founder reviews each message, fills in the brackets, and sends it from their own account only after
the team agrees.

Rules for every message:

- Say plainly that we're a high school student team. Don't hide it and don't lead with it as a gimmick.
- No invented numbers, customers, logos, endorsements or testimonials. No "trusted by," no "AI lawyer."
- Don't claim time savings. We don't know them yet; the pilot is how we find out.
- One follow-up every 5 to 7 business days, at most two. A "no" or no reply after the second follow-up ends it.
- Use only contact routes the firm publishes (its email or contact form). Never guess an email address.

## 90-second demo script

Use the demo button on `/pro`. Every case, name and document is fictional.

| Time | Screen | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Cases list | "This is a fictional firm with three inherited-home matters. One is complete, one is missing documents, and one has conflicts." |
| 0:10 | Open DEMO-2026-02 (Okafor) | "The checklist shows what's still missing: the death certificate and the deed. Your attorney approves this checklist once for the firm." |
| 0:20 | Family links | "Staff create a private link for a family member. It expires in 14 days and shows only what that person was asked for." |
| 0:30 | Open the link in a second tab | "This is what the family sees on a phone. No account. They upload a photo or PDF, or type the family members." |
| 0:45 | Open DEMO-2026-03 (Lindqvist), Review | "HomeKeep read these fictional PDFs and suggested facts, each with the page it came from. Here the death certificate says March 3 and the family typed March 13. It flags that instead of picking one." |
| 1:00 | Warning on the tax bill | "This file had a line telling software to approve everything. HomeKeep treats uploads as untrusted, so it flags that line for staff." |
| 1:10 | Approve a fact, export the packet | "Staff approve or correct each fact. The packet lists documents, approved facts with sources, conflicts and missing items, marked for professional review." |
| 1:25 | Back to cases | "It doesn't give legal advice or decide heirs. We're looking for a few firms to try it for 30 days. Could we learn how intake works in your office?" |

## Longer demo outline (20 minutes)

1. Two minutes on how they handle intake today. Let them talk first.
2. Walk the fictional Okafor case from opening to family link (5 minutes).
3. Lindqvist review screen: evidence, conflicts, the prompt-injection warning, corrections and history (5 minutes).
4. Packet export and what it leaves for the attorney (3 minutes).
5. Security page: what is and isn't in place, read honestly, including the blockers (3 minutes).
6. Pilot terms and what we'd ask of them (2 minutes). Ask whether it's worth a second conversation; don't push for a
   yes on the call.

> "Hi, my name is [first name]. I'm one of four high school students in Columbus who built a free website about

If the receptionist says no, thank them and mark the firm "declined" in the tracker. Don't call again.

## Discovery call guide (20 to 30 minutes)

Goal: learn how intake works, not pitch. Take notes in the tracker. Don't record a call without everyone's
permission at the start.

Opening: thank them, say you're students, that this is research, and ask if 20 minutes still works.

Ask, and let them talk:

1. Walk me through the last estate you handled where a house was involved. Who sent what, and how?
2. Which documents are hardest to get from families? Why?
3. Who on your team chases them? Roughly how much time does that take per matter?
4. How do documents arrive today: email, mail, drop-off, a portal?
5. What happens when two documents disagree, like a date or a name?
6. How many matters a month involve a house? (Their estimate; write down that it's an estimate.)
7. What software do you use for cases and documents? What do you like and dislike about it?
8. What would make you nervous about families uploading documents to a new tool?
9. If something cut the chasing in half, what would that be worth to you? (Listen; don't anchor on our price.)
10. Who else decides on new tools at your firm?

Close: ask if they'd like the demo, or if we can follow up after we've talked to a few more firms. Ask who else we
should talk to. Thank them.

After the call, record in the tracker within a day: what they said, their own words for the problem, any numbers they
gave (marked as their estimate), objections, and the next step they agreed to.

Signs of real demand: they describe a recent painful case without prompting, someone on staff spends real time on
it, and they ask about price or a start date. Signs of weak demand: "interesting," no time estimate, nobody owns the
problem.
