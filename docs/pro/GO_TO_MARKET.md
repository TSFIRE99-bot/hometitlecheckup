# Finding the first pilot firms

Nothing in this plan has started. No firm has been contacted.

## Prospect list and tracker

`docs/pro/prospects.csv` lists 30 Ohio firms (25 in Franklin County) and 5 legal aid and housing organizations to
ask for advice, not to sell to. Every row was checked against the firm's own website or a named directory on
November 17, 2026. Emails appear only where the firm publishes them; everything else says "unknown" or points to the
firm's contact form. Rows 25 to 29 come from directories only and need their official websites found before any
contact. Ranking is by documented fit: a probate or estate administration page, Franklin County, real estate work,
and a small office.

Open the CSV in Google Sheets or Excel and keep it there. Update `stage`, `last_contact` and `next_step` after every
touch. Stages, in order:

researched → contacted → replied → discovery scheduled → qualified → demo completed → pilot offered → pilot accepted
→ active pilot → renewed

Any row can move to **declined** at any point. "Qualified" means the firm described a recent inherited-home case with
real document chasing and someone who owns the problem.

## Weekly plan (goals, not predictions)

| Week | Outreach | Conversations | Demos | Pilots |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Founders approve the drafts and the top 10 firms; send 10 first emails or form messages | 0 to 2 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | First follow-ups; 10 more first contacts | 3 to 5 | 1 | 0 |
| 3 | Second follow-ups; last 10 first contacts | 5 to 8 | 2 to 3 | Offer to qualified firms |
| 4 | Advice calls with legal aid and housing groups | 10 total | 4 total | 2 accepted is the stretch goal |

The targets (10 conversations, 4 demos, 2 paid pilots) are goals for the month. If week 3 ends with fewer than 4
conversations, stop adding firms and rewrite the first email; the message or the list is wrong. If 10 conversations
produce no qualified firm, write that down plainly: it means the problem, the buyer or the timing is wrong, not that
we need more outreach.

No paid ads. Channels: the firms' published contact routes, warm introductions from people we already know, and the
legal aid and housing advisers (who may know which firms handle these cases).

## Measuring a pilot

Before the pilot starts, ask the firm for a baseline on its last three inherited-home matters: rough staff minutes
spent chasing documents, number of follow-ups, and days from opening to "ready for the attorney." Write down that these
are the firm's estimates.

During the pilot, HomeKeep's **Settings > Pilot measures** panel counts cases opened, packets exported and the median
days from opening to packet, files sent by families versus staff, follow-ups recorded, and facts reviewed and
corrected. Track by hand: staff minutes per matter (a one-line note per matter), our support time, our costs, and
whether staff still use it in weeks 3 and 4.

In the end-of-pilot report, compare against the firm's own baseline and say clearly that it's a small before-and-after
comparison, not proof that HomeKeep caused any change. Report what didn't work too.

## Case studies

Ask only after the pilot ends, in writing, and only for what the firm approves word for word. Offer an anonymous
version ("a small Franklin County probate office"). Saying no changes nothing about their pilot or price. Never
include client names, case details or documents, even with permission from the firm.

## Referrals

At the end of every conversation, ask "Who else should we talk to?" Write down who suggested whom. Don't offer
payment for referrals, and never rank or recommend firms to families based on whether they pay HomeKeep. The free
checkup's free-help list stays as it is: legal aid and official offices only.

## Lines we don't cross

- No selling or sharing homeowner information, and no prospect lists built from vulnerable households.
- The free checkup never feeds the Pro workspace and never enrolls visitors in marketing.
- Free help is never conditioned on using a paying firm.
- No share of any home's equity or sale.
